LEGAL DOCUMENT PREPARATION

Keep Your Important Legal Documents Safe

Knowing where your important documents are can make a difficult moment easier to manage.

← Back to Legal Insights

Important legal documents are often prepared for significant events or transactions. Yet, when the time comes to use them, people may discover that the original cannot be located, the document is incomplete, or no one knows where it was kept.

A little organisation can make a real difference. Keeping your documents secure and making sure the right person knows how to locate them can help avoid unnecessary delays and stress.

Documents you may need to keep safely

  • Your Will: Keep the original in a secure place where it can be located when required. Let your executor or a trusted person know where it is kept, without compromising its security.
  • Property documents: Keep deeds, conveyances, agreements for sale, mortgage documents, plans and related correspondence together and properly identified.
  • Powers of Attorney: Retain the executed document and any relevant registration details. Make sure the person appointed knows what authority has been given.
  • Estate and probate documents: Keep Grants of Probate, Letters of Administration, death certificates and documents relating to the administration of an estate in an organised file.
  • Other important records: Depending on your circumstances, this may include marriage certificates, birth certificates, court orders, declarations and financial records.

Practical ways to organise your documents

Choose a secure storage location

Use a location that protects documents from damage, loss and unauthorised access. Consider risks such as fire, water, theft and accidental disposal.

Keep documents organised

Use clearly labelled folders or files. Separate original documents from photocopies and make a simple record of what is stored and where.

Let someone trustworthy know

A document is of limited practical use if no one can find it when it is needed. Consider telling your executor or another trusted person where important originals are kept and how they may be accessed.

Review your records periodically

When you acquire property, prepare a new Will, execute a Power of Attorney or complete an important legal transaction, update your records. Remove outdated copies from active files while preserving documents that should be retained.

Take care with digital copies

Scanned copies can be useful for reference and backup, but they may not replace an original document where the law or the institution dealing with it requires the original or a certified copy. Store digital records securely and protect accounts with strong passwords and appropriate access controls.

Keep your important documents safe, organised and easy for the right person to locate.

A simple document system today can help your family, representatives or attorney-at-law find the information they need when it matters.
