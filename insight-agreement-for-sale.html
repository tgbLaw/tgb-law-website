Before You Sign That Agreement for Sale

Buying property? Understand the agreement before you commit.

← Back to Legal Insights

Purchasing a property is one of the most significant financial commitments many people will make. While finding the right property is exciting, it is equally important to understand the legal arrangements before signing an Agreement for Sale.

An Agreement for Sale sets out the terms upon which the vendor agrees to sell and the purchaser agrees to buy the property. It should clearly record the parties' agreement and address the arrangements leading to completion.

Six important matters to consider before signing

1. Check the parties' details

Ensure that the names and identifying details of the vendor and purchaser are accurate. Where a party is a company or someone is acting under a Power of Attorney, the capacity and authority to enter into the transaction should also be considered.

2. Make sure the property is correctly described

The agreement should properly identify the property being purchased. Your attorney should examine the relevant title documents and investigate the vendor's ability to sell.

In Trinidad and Tobago, land may be held under the deeds system or the registered land title system. The appropriate title investigation will depend on the system applicable to the property.

3. Understand the purchase price and deposit

Check the agreed purchase price, the deposit payable, when payments are due and how the balance will be settled. Make sure you understand what happens to the deposit if the transaction does not proceed.

4. Review the conditions and inspection arrangements

If the purchase is subject to mortgage approval, a satisfactory inspection or other conditions, these should be properly addressed in the agreement. Consider whether you need time to inspect the property with a suitably qualified professional.

5. Pay attention to the completion date

The agreement should provide a realistic timeframe for completion, taking into account mortgage arrangements, searches, documentation and any other requirements. An unrealistic completion date can create unnecessary pressure and potential difficulties.

6. Obtain independent legal advice

Have your own Attorney-at-Law review the agreement before signing. Your attorney can explain the terms, identify potential concerns, investigate the title and advise you on the steps required to complete the transaction.

An important point in Trinidad and Tobago

There are statutory requirements governing the form and registration of agreements for the sale of land. Under section 15B of the Registration of Deeds Act, Chap. 19:06, as amended by the Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 2020, the attorney-at-law who prepares the agreement is generally required to register it within 30 days of full execution, subject to the statutory provisions.

A property transaction involves more than agreeing on a price. The agreement, title investigation, financing arrangements and completion documentation all play an important role.

Before you sign, make sure you understand what you are agreeing to.
