LEGAL DOCUMENT PREPARATION

When Might You Need a Power of Attorney?

A Power of Attorney can allow someone you trust to act on your behalf for specified matters.

← Back to Legal Insights

There may be times when you need someone else to handle a transaction or certain affairs on your behalf. You may be living overseas, travelling, unable to attend a particular appointment or managing several commitments. A Power of Attorney may provide a legal way for a trusted person to act for you within the authority you grant.

What is a Power of Attorney?

A Power of Attorney is a legal instrument by which one person (the donor) authorises another person (the donee or attorney) to act on the donor’s behalf in relation to the matters set out in the document. The word “attorney” in this context refers to the person appointed under the document; it does not necessarily mean an Attorney-at-Law.

When might it be useful?

A Power of Attorney may be considered where you are unable to attend to a transaction in person or where you need someone to carry out specified tasks for you. For example, it may be used in connection with:

  • managing a particular property transaction in Trinidad and Tobago while you are overseas;
  • signing documents or completing steps connected with a sale or purchase, where the authority is properly granted;
  • handling specified banking or financial matters, subject to the requirements of the institution; or
  • attending to another defined legal or administrative matter on your behalf.

Choose the person and the powers carefully

A Power of Attorney can grant significant authority. The person appointed should be trustworthy, responsible and capable of carrying out the task. The document should clearly state what the person is authorised to do. Depending on your needs, the authority may be limited to a particular transaction or may cover a wider range of matters.

The donee must act within the authority granted and should act in the donor’s interests. A Power of Attorney should not be treated as a blank cheque or given to someone simply because they are available.

Formal requirements matter

In Trinidad and Tobago, a Power of Attorney is generally prepared and executed as a deed, and registration requirements may apply. Section 20 of the Registration of Deeds Act, Chap. 19:06, addresses the execution and registration of Powers of Attorney for use in legal proceedings. Additional requirements may apply where the document concerns registered land or a particular transaction. The relevant requirements should be checked before the document is signed or relied upon.

Important limitations

A Power of Attorney is not a substitute for a Will and does not authorise someone to deal with your affairs after your death. It also does not automatically give a person authority to make every decision for you. The scope and continuing effect of the document depend on the law and the circumstances. If a person has already lost the mental capacity required to grant a Power of Attorney, a different legal process may be necessary.

A carefully prepared Power of Attorney can be useful, but it is important to understand exactly what authority you are giving and to whom.

Obtain legal advice before executing a Power of Attorney, particularly where property, substantial funds or important personal affairs are involved.
