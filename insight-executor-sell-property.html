Can an Executor Sell Property Before the Grant of Probate?

Being named as an executor does not mean you can immediately deal with every estate asset.

← Back to Legal Insights

This is a question that often arises after someone dies leaving a Will that names an executor. Family members may wish to sell a property, transfer ownership or use funds from the estate to meet expenses. However, being named as an executor in a Will and having the legal authority to administer the estate are not the same thing.

What is the Grant of Probate?

A Grant of Probate is issued by the Probate Registry and confirms the executor’s authority to administer the deceased person’s estate in accordance with the Will and the law. The executor named in the Will must apply for the Grant and satisfy the requirements of the Probate Registry.

Can the executor sell the property before the Grant?

As a general rule, an executor should not assume that being named in the Will gives them authority to sell or transfer estate property before the Grant of Probate has been issued. The Administration of Estates Act, Chap. 9:01, provides for the vesting of a deceased person’s estate in law in the Administrator General pending the grant of Probate or Letters of Administration, subject to the statutory framework.

There are limited statutory provisions and circumstances that may affect what can be done before a Grant. These should not be treated as a general permission for an executor or family member to sell estate property without first obtaining advice.

What happens after the Grant is issued?

Once the Grant is issued, the personal representative can take the steps authorised by law to collect, manage and administer the estate. This may include dealing with land, paying lawful debts and expenses, and distributing or transferring assets in accordance with the Will and the applicable law.

The precise steps depend on the title to the property, the terms of the Will, the number of executors and the circumstances of the estate. For example, where there is more than one executor, the authority to sell or transfer real estate may require particular attention to the statutory rules and the terms of the Grant.

What should beneficiaries and family members do?

  • Locate the original Will and identify the executor or executors named in it.
  • Obtain legal advice before signing an agreement or attempting to sell or transfer estate property.
  • Allow the executor to make the appropriate application to the Probate Registry.
  • Keep records of estate assets, expenses and communications with the personal representative.
Do not assume that a named executor can sell estate property immediately after a death. The Grant and the applicable legal requirements matter.

Probate is an important step in the lawful administration of an estate. Getting advice early can help prevent disputes, delays and transactions that may later be challenged.
