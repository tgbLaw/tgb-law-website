Family Matters: Not Every Dispute Needs to Be Contested

Where it is appropriate and safe, discussion and mediation may help families find a way forward.

← Back to Legal Insights

Family disputes can be emotionally difficult. When disagreements arise about children, finances, property or separation, court proceedings may seem like the only way forward. However, not every family matter has to become a fully contested dispute.

In appropriate circumstances, parties may be able to resolve some or all of their issues through communication, negotiation or mediation. The best approach will depend on the nature of the dispute and the circumstances of the people involved.

What is family mediation?

Mediation is a process in which a neutral third party helps people communicate, identify the issues in dispute and explore possible solutions. The mediator does not decide the outcome for the parties. Any agreement is reached voluntarily by the parties themselves.

The Judiciary’s Family Court offers mediation services for suitable family disputes. These may include matters involving children, such as schooling, living arrangements and expenses, as well as separation, property and financial issues. The Family Proceedings Act, Chap. 46:09, provides for a court referral to mediation in appropriate circumstances with the agreement of the parties.

When might mediation be helpful?

  • Parenting arrangements: Parents may need to discuss a child’s routine, schooling, communication and practical arrangements.
  • Maintenance and expenses: Parties may be able to discuss the financial needs of a child and how expenses should be met.
  • Property and financial matters: Where both parties are willing to engage, mediation may help identify areas of agreement and narrow the issues that remain unresolved.
  • Communication difficulties: A neutral mediator may help keep discussions focused and constructive.

Is mediation suitable for every situation?

No. Mediation is not appropriate in every case. Where there are safety concerns, intimidation, abuse, a significant imbalance of power or an urgent need for court protection, a different approach may be necessary. The suitability of mediation should be considered carefully, and legal advice remains important.

Even where mediation does not resolve every issue, it may help the parties identify what is agreed and what still requires a decision by the court.

Get advice before making decisions

Before agreeing to arrangements involving children, property or financial responsibilities, make sure you understand the legal implications. An agreement may need to be properly documented or approved by the court, depending on the issue.

The goal is not simply to avoid court. It is to find a fair, safe and practical way to address the issues that matter.

Every family situation is different. The appropriate process should protect the interests and wellbeing of those involved, especially children.
