When Was Your Will Last Reviewed?

Life changes. Make sure your Will still reflects your wishes.

← Back to Legal Insights

Making a Will is an important step in planning for the future. But preparing a Will is not necessarily the end of the process. As life changes, the arrangements you made some years ago may no longer reflect your wishes or your circumstances.

A review gives you an opportunity to check that the people you have appointed and the instructions you have included are still appropriate.

When should you consider reviewing your Will?

Marriage or a change in relationship circumstances

Marriage is particularly important. Under section 48 of the Wills and Probate Act, Chap. 9:03, marriage generally revokes an existing Will, subject to statutory exceptions. If you marry after making a Will, obtain advice about whether a new Will is required.

Changes in your family

The birth or adoption of a child, the death of a beneficiary, or a change in your family relationships may affect how you want your estate to be distributed. You may also wish to reconsider who you have appointed to act as executor.

Changes in your assets or financial circumstances

You may have purchased or sold property, acquired investments, started a business or received an inheritance. Check whether your Will adequately deals with your current assets and whether any gifts or instructions need to be reconsidered.

Changes in the people you have appointed

Consider whether your chosen executor is still willing and able to carry out the role. You may also wish to review any guardianship provisions or other arrangements included in your Will.

Do not make informal changes

Writing notes on your Will, crossing out provisions or making handwritten amendments may not achieve the legal result you intend. Changes to a Will must be made in accordance with the legal requirements. Depending on the circumstances, a new Will or a properly executed codicil may be appropriate.

It is also important to remember that a change in circumstances does not automatically revoke a Will simply because your wishes may have changed. The legal effect of a particular event should be considered before you rely on an old document.

A Will should be reviewed whenever an important change in your life makes you question whether it still reflects your wishes.

Taking the time to review your Will can help reduce uncertainty and make your intentions clearer for the people who may have to administer your estate.
